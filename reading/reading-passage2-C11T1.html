Growing crops in the city

Passage 1 — Crop-growing skyscrapers

By the year 2050, nearly 80% of the Earth's population will live in urban areas. This means that huge amounts of land are needed to feed city dwellers. Currently, agriculture takes up 38% of the Earth's land surface. If we continue with traditional farming methods, we will need an area the size of Brazil to feed the world's population by 2050. This is simply not possible.

One possible solution is vertical farming — growing crops in skyscrapers in the heart of the city. Vertical farms would use hydroponic systems, where plants are grown in water with added nutrients rather than soil. This uses 90% less water than traditional farming. Crops would be grown under special lights, allowing harvests all year round, completely independent of weather and seasons.

Scientists estimate that one vertical farm occupying a 30-storey building with a base of just one hectare could produce the same amount of food as 400 hectares of traditional farmland. Pesticides would not be needed because crops would be grown in a controlled environment free from pests. Transport costs and pollution would be greatly reduced because food would be consumed close to where it is grown.
