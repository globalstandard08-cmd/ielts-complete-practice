Reading Passage 3 — The Psychology of Innovation

The Psychology of Innovation

A

Why are so few companies truly innovative? Innovation is key to business survival, and companies spend millions of dollars encouraging it. Management gurus, consultants and academics have all had their theories — but the fact remains that for every highly successful innovative company, there are hundreds of others that fail. The problem, according to a growing number of experts, is that we have misunderstood what innovation actually is. We think it is about technology, or products, or processes — but it is actually about people.

B

One of the most famous examples of innovation is the ‘Million Dollar Quartet’ — a 1956 recording session that brought together Elvis Presley, Johnny Cash, Carl Perkins and Jerry Lee Lewis. These four young musicians went on to change the face of popular music. But what made them so special? It wasn’t just their individual talent. Sam Phillips, the owner of Sun Records, had a clear vision: he wanted to create a new kind of music. He didn’t just hire talented people; he brought together people who shared his ambition. Roy Orbison, a talented singer who was also at Sun Records at the time, was not part of the group — because he didn’t share that goal. As Phillips later said: ‘They knew exactly what we were trying to do, and they wanted to do it too.’

C

Innovation thrives when people work together towards a shared objective. Gary Hamel, a leading expert on management, argues that successful innovation is rarely the work of a single genius. He points to the example of the team that developed the first personal computer at Apple. The team included engineers, designers, marketers and even a psychologist. None of them was the ‘best’ in the world at what they did — but together they created something that changed the world. ‘The most innovative companies,’ Hamel says, ‘are not the ones with the smartest people. They are the ones that create the environment where smart people can work together effectively.’

D

So what makes a good innovative team? Research shows that the best teams are those where members are aware of their own limitations. Bill Gates once said: ‘We all have blind spots. The key is to find people whose strengths complement your weaknesses.’ In the early days of Microsoft, Gates and his partner Paul Allen deliberately hired people who were smarter than them — but who also knew what they didn’t know. Unlike many leaders, who surround themselves with people who agree with them, Gates and Allen wanted people who would challenge their ideas. This, they believed, was the only way to create something truly new.

E

Another important factor is commitment. Psychologists have found that when people write down their goals or ideas, they are much more likely to achieve them. This is why some companies ask employees to write short statements on cereal packets or posters and display them in the office. This simple act makes people feel more responsible for their ideas and more committed to making them work. It also helps to align everyone’s thinking towards the same purpose.

F

However, even the best teams can fail if the environment is wrong. Research by Harvard Business School shows that physical surroundings have surprisingly little effect on creativity. Many companies spend huge amounts of money designing ‘innovation centres’ with open-plan offices, bright colours and relaxing spaces — only to find that their employees are no more creative than before. What matters far more is the psychological environment. If people feel safe to take risks, to make mistakes and to express their ideas, they will be creative. If they feel judged or controlled, they will not.

G

Change is also a major factor. When things change, people naturally become cautious. Evolution has programmed us to ‘play it safe’ when we feel uncertain. This is why innovation often slows down during times of change. But psychologists have also found that people are more likely to take risks when they are threatened with a loss than when they are offered a reward. This means that leaders who want to encourage innovation should focus on what might be lost if they don’t change, rather than what might be gained.

H

One of the biggest enemies of innovation is ‘captainitis’ — a term used to describe what happens when a leader is too dominant. When one person makes all the decisions and controls everything, other team members stop contributing. They feel that their ideas don’t matter, so they stop sharing them. The best leaders, by contrast, encourage everyone to speak up. They make sure that every contribution is valued, and they use their power to support the team, not to control it. As one CEO put it: ‘My job is not to have all the answers. My job is to make sure everyone else’s answers are heard.’
