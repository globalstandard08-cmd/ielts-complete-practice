Words: 0 / 150 minimum

✅ Model Answer (Band 9)

The two pie charts illustrate the proportion of energy consumed in a typical Australian home and the corresponding percentage of greenhouse gas emissions produced from each activity. Overall, heating accounts for the largest share of energy use, while water heating is responsible for the highest amount of greenhouse gases. In contrast, cooling and lighting represent the smallest proportions in both categories. Looking at energy consumption in detail, heating makes up 42% of total usage, followed by water heating at 30%. Other appliances use 15%, while refrigeration, lighting, and cooling account for 7%, 4%, and only 2% respectively. When it comes to emissions, the pattern is different. Water heating produces 32% of greenhouse gases, slightly more than heating at 15%. Other appliances generate 28% of emissions, which is nearly double their share of energy use. Refrigeration and lighting contribute 14% and 8% respectively, while cooling remains the lowest at just 3%.

📝 Writing Task 2

⏱️ Suggested time: 40 minutes 📏 Minimum: 250 words
Question:

It is important for children to learn the difference between right and wrong at an early age. Punishment is necessary to help them learn this distinction.

To what extent do you agree or disagree with this opinion?

What sort of punishment should parents and teachers be allowed to use to teach good behaviour to children?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience.
Words: 0 / 250 minimum

✅ Model Answer (Band 9)

Many people believe that children must understand right from wrong from a young age, and that punishment is an essential part of this process. While I agree that guidance is necessary, I do not believe that all forms of punishment are helpful, and it is important to choose appropriate methods. On one hand, setting clear boundaries helps children develop a sense of responsibility. Without any consequences for their actions, they may grow up believing that there are no limits to what they can do. This can lead to poor behaviour later in life. However, harsh or physical punishment is not only ineffective but can also damage a child’s self-esteem and cause fear rather than understanding. On the other hand, there are better alternatives to teach values. Logical consequences work well: for example, if a child breaks something, they help repair or replace it. Similarly, removing privileges such as screen time or playtime can be effective without causing harm. When combined with explanation, these methods help children understand why their actions were wrong, not just that they will be punished. In conclusion, while children do need guidance to learn right from wrong, punishment should be reasonable and educational. It should aim to teach, not to hurt. Used correctly, it can help children grow into responsible adults.
